# Look

A look is what makes every frame of a film recognisably that film. It's a set of decisions about how things are drawn,
lit and coloured, held consistently and shifted on purpose.

## A style needs structure

A filter on top of ordinary renders isn't a style. A style is built from a few choices that run through everything:

- **Shading:** realistic (physically based materials), stylized realistic (simplified forms, real light), toon or cel
  (stepped light bands), flat (no shading at all), painterly (brush texture in the shading).
- **Edges and line:** none, ink outlines, hand-drawn wobbly lines, line only where forms turn.
- **Texture:** clean, grainy, printed (halftone dots, screen print), paper, fabric, film grain.
- **Shape language:** round and soft, angular and sharp, blocky, organic.
- **Colour rules:** limited palette, one accent, saturated world with a muted subject or the reverse.
- **Light rules:** hard bands, soft gradients, rim-lit silhouettes, deep shadows.
- **Type:** how words look when they appear (`craft/type.md`).

Write the four or five choices that define this film's look in `treatment/look.md`, and prove them in look frames before
building shots.

## Consistency and shifts

The look holds across the film so it reads as one piece. It can shift for the story (a section in a different palette, a
memory in a different texture, the turn changing the whole world), but each shift is planned, motivated and keeps
enough constant (the line, the shape language, the type) that it's still the same film.

## Techniques: more than one

Different moments want different tools. A film that uses one technique for every shot feels samey, however varied its
props. Choose per section or per line:

- **3D sets and characters:** space, camera moves through real places, light.
- **2D animation** (drawn, cut-out, flat graphics, Grease Pencil in Blender): memory, imagination, emphasis, a break in
  texture; it can live inside the 3D world (on a wall, a screen, a page) or take over the frame.
- **Particles:** dust, sparks, crowds of small things, words or objects dissolving and forming, swarms.
- **Simulation:** cloth that moves, liquid that pours and floods, things that fall and shatter, smoke and fire. Real
  physical behaviour makes a moment feel tangible.
- **Procedural geometry** (geometry nodes): cities that grow, patterns, things that assemble themselves, huge repeated
  structures.
- **Shader-driven looks:** a material that changes (wood to stone, colour draining away), a dissolve along a pattern,
  stylized rendering, screens, holograms, x-ray views.
- **Type as image:** words built into the world, or graphics that carry a passage.
- **Compositing:** layering 2D over 3D, mattes, split screens, a world inside a shape.

The treatment names the techniques it will use and where. Mixing them is a strength when each one is chosen for what
it says.

## Detail

Detail at several scales (big shapes, medium forms, small surface detail) makes a frame feel real and rich at any style.
"Simple", "minimal" or "stylized" are choices about how detail is drawn, not permission to leave a frame empty. Every
surface gets a real material: in a realistic style, real textures (`craft/sets.md` lists free sources); in a stylized
one, a material designed for the style.

## Proving a look

Build two or three frames of real moments from the film, lit and finished, at full resolution, before planning shots
around the look. A look described in words and never rendered is a guess. Look at each frame full size. If a frame
doesn't look like a still from a film you'd want to watch, the look isn't ready.
