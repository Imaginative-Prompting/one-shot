# Research

When a story is about the real world (current events, a scene, a person, a technology, a history), the lyric's deep cuts
and the film's references have to be true and current. Your own knowledge stops months before today and blurs dates;
anything recent comes from research, with its date checked.

## How much

For the song, little: the story's few big moments and what people say about them. A song carries a handful of
references, so research that finds seventy is wasted and tempts the lyric into a list. The picture can use more (on
screens, signs, in the background), researched when the film needs it.

## What to find

- **The story's facts:** what happened, when, who was involved, the numbers that matter, what is confirmed and what is
  reported or alleged.
- **What people actually talk about:** the moments, names and phrases that everyone following the topic knows: what the
  big outlets led with, what the explainers and recap channels covered, what became a meme. A high score on one niche
  site isn't enough; if it never reached people's feeds, it isn't a deep cut anyone will catch.
- **How people say it:** nicknames, running jokes, the words the community uses rather than press-release language.
- **The history and comparisons** the story leans on (an earlier era, a precedent), with their own dates and numbers.
- **The look of the real things** the film will show: logos, products, places, people's recognisable features, so they
  can be built true to life.

## How

- Search the web for each thread, naming the month and year so results are current; check every date against today's.
  When a search summary disagrees with itself or mixes years, open the sources.
- Read the primary source when there is one (the company's post, the filing, the paper, the person's own words); take
  what several outlets agree on when there isn't.
- Captions of recap videos and the top comments of big discussion threads show how people talk about it.
- Keep downloaded pages, captions and screenshots in `films/<slug>/research/ref/`, for matching only: the film recreates
  everything it shows.

## The fact sheet

`films/<slug>/research/facts.md`: one table per thread, each fact with an id, its date, its source, and a mark (P: read
at the primary source; S: several outlets agree; 1: one outlet). Anything alleged or reported says so in the fact itself.
End with what you looked at and left out, and why.

## Facts in the song and the film

- A fact sung as true is true, dated and in the fact sheet. An allegation is sung as an allegation ("they say", "word is",
  "nobody's proved it").
- The picture can exaggerate, joke and invent: satire, caricature and visual metaphor are the film's job. The lyric
  carries the facts; the film is free to play with them.
- A real person's recognisable features (glasses, hair, signature clothes) can be drawn; their face can be left blank or
  stylised. Don't invent events about a real person in the lyric.
