# Transitions

A transition is a sentence's punctuation: it tells the viewer how two moments relate. A good one is motivated by
something in the picture or the music, short, and invisible or deliberate, never accidental.

## Cuts

- **Straight cut:** the default between shots. Works best between shots that differ clearly in size or angle; a cut
  between nearly identical framings reads as a mistake (a jump cut), which is only right when the jump is the effect.
- **Cut on action:** cut during a movement (a turn, a throw, a step) and continue it in the next shot. The motion hides
  the cut.
- **Match cut:** the next shot repeats a shape, a motion, a colour or a composition from the last (a spinning wheel to a
  spinning record, a face to the moon). It links two ideas. Strong at a section change or a time jump.
- **Smash cut:** an abrupt cut from one extreme to its opposite (loud to silent, chaos to calm). Rare and on a hard
  musical moment.
- **On the music:** cuts land on beats or just before them, and the biggest cuts on downbeats and section changes. Cutting
  on every beat is exhausting; vary the rhythm with the music's density.

## Without a cut

- **Whip:** a fast pan or move with heavy motion blur, landing in the next composition. A quick, eased move of about a
  third to half a second, not a slow truck stretched to fill time. It can hide a cut in the middle of the blur.
- **Through an object:** the camera passes through something that fills the frame (a doorway, a dark shape, a screen,
  a wall, a crowd) and comes out in a new place. The swap happens while the frame is covered by real geometry.
- **Into a shape:** the camera pushes into a window, a circle, an eye, a screen, and the next world appears inside it and
  grows past the frame's edges. It reads as one world opening into another.
- **Foreground wipe:** something passes close to the lens and wipes the frame; behind it, the scene has changed.
- **Match move:** a shape or motion continues across two worlds while everything else changes around it.
- **Morph:** one object becomes another. Strongest when the two are linked in meaning, not just in shape.
- **The hero goes through first.** When a character leads the transition, they go through ahead of the camera and are
  found on the other side at the same place and size in frame, so the eye never loses them.

## Dissolves and fades

A dissolve says time has passed or two moments belong together; a fade to black ends something. Both are soft and slow,
so they feel dated or lazy when used to avoid designing a transition. A flash of white or a quick fade used to hide a
swap is the weakest transition there is: it covers the change rather than making it meaningful.

## Graphic transitions

Type, shapes or 2D graphics that sweep across and change the scene: a word that fills the frame and becomes the next
world, a shape that expands, a split screen. They fit graphic and typographic films, and can be very strong at a
section change in a 3D film when they belong to its style.

## Choosing

Ask what the two shots have in common (a shape, a motion, an object, a colour, an idea) and build the transition from
that. When they share nothing, a clean cut on the beat is better than a decorative effect. Use the biggest, most
inventive transitions for the biggest changes (sections, the turn), and keep the rest clean. Planned transitions go on
the shot cards: the outgoing shot's last frames and the incoming shot's first are designed together.
